FIB-SEM tomography

A focused ion beam (FIB) removes a slice of material a few tens of nanometres thick, then the scanning electron microscope (SEM) images the freshly exposed face. Repeating this hundreds of times gives a stack of images that can be segmented and reconstructed in 3D. Here the sample is a solid electrolyte containing lithium filaments (green), voids (blue) and Ag-rich particles (amber).

  • Press Mill and watch the reconstruction build up slice by slice. Drag it to rotate.
  • Make the slices thicker: the run is faster, but the 3D model gets coarser along the milling direction.
  • Shorten the dwell time and switch to Segmented: noisier images give a noisier segmentation. Compare SE (topographic edges) with BSE (composition) contrast.

Model: an analytic 3D volume, "imaged" with approximate SE/BSE contrast, curtaining and shot noise, then segmented by thresholding a smoothed image. Detector and dwell-time changes apply to slices milled afterwards.

4D-STEM

A focused electron probe is scanned across the sample and a full 2D diffraction pattern is recorded at every probe position, giving a four-dimensional dataset. Afterwards, any "virtual detector" can be applied in software to make an image: bright field, annular dark field, a dark-field image from a single Bragg spot, or a map of crystal orientation.

  • Drag the probe across the sample (in the 3D view or the left panel) and watch the diffraction pattern change between grains and the amorphous region.
  • Switch virtual detectors, or click a Bragg spot to make a dark-field image of only the grain that produces it.
  • Increase the convergence angle until the disks overlap, as in ptychography, then add defocus to see shadow images of the atoms inside the disks.

Model: single-slice phase object. Exit wave = probe × transmission function, diffraction pattern = |FFT|². 300 kV electrons, 0.25 Å pixels, 128 × 128-pixel patterns on a 40 × 40 scan.

Real material: LLZO garnet electrolyte, simulated with abTEM

The same experiment on a real solid electrolyte: tetragonal Li7La3Zr2O12 viewed down the [001] axis. These diffraction patterns were computed ahead of time with full multislice simulations, which track the electron wave as it scatters through a 10 nm-thick crystal, and are loaded into the viewer.

  • At 1 mrad, the disks are small and separate: this is the LLZO diffraction pattern. Click a spot to make a dark-field image.
  • At 20 mrad, the probe is smaller than the spacing between atom columns. The virtual images resolve the La and Zr columns, and the annular bright-field detector picks up the light O and Li columns as well.
  • Move the probe onto a La column and off it again, and watch how the intensity inside the central disk shifts.

Structure: Materials Project mp-942733 (I41/acd, a = 13.24 Å, c = 12.70 Å, DFT-relaxed). Simulated with abTEM (Madsen & Susi, Open Research Europe 2021): 300 kV, Lobato potentials, 2.1 Å slices, no defocus or aberrations, and no thermal diffuse scattering, so dark-field contrast is lower than in a real experiment. Patterns are cropped and stored on a log scale.

EELS

As fast electrons pass through the sample, some lose energy by exciting plasmons or knocking out core electrons. In the energy-loss spectrum each element shows an absorption edge at a characteristic energy, and the shape of the edge reflects bonding. Here the probe sits on a Li metal | interphase | garnet (LLZO) solid-electrolyte interface.

  • Drag the probe across the interface and watch the Li K, C K, O K and La M4,5 edges appear and disappear.
  • Open the low-loss window and increase the thickness: multiple plasmon peaks pile up and edges get smeared.
  • Acquire a spectrum image. Under each edge, the background is fitted with a power law (A·E−r) and subtracted to map each element.

Model: zero-loss peak plus Poisson-weighted plasmons, power-law background, and schematic edge shapes and cross-sections, with counting noise.
